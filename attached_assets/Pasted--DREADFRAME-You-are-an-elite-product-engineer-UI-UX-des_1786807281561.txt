# DREADFRAME

You are an elite product engineer, UI/UX designer, creative technologist, and AI application architect.

Your job is to design and build a polished web application called **DREADFRAME**, an AI-powered horror story development and cinematic storyboard studio.

Do not create a generic AI wrapper.

The product should feel like a professional creative tool combining the atmosphere of an independent horror film, the usability of a modern creative SaaS product, and the visual workflow of a director's storyboard room.

Make sensible product decisions autonomously when requirements are ambiguous. Prioritize a functional, impressive MVP over unnecessary complexity.

---

# 1. PRODUCT VISION

DREADFRAME helps users turn themselves, a photograph, or a simple idea into an original cinematic horror story.

The core journey is:

**Face / Photo / Idea → Horror Concept → Character → Story Arc → Sequences → Shots → Storyboard → Visual Frames**

Users should feel like they are co-directing a horror film with an AI creative director.

The application should help users create:

* horror concepts
* themes
* subgenres
* characters
* protagonist psychology
* macro character arcs
* micro narrative arcs
* horror rules
* plot structures
* sequences
* scenes
* cinematic shots
* storyboard frames
* visual prompts
* alternate story variations
* alternate endings

---

# 2. BUILD TWO EXPERIENCES

Build:

1. A public marketing landing page.
2. An authenticated-style creative application called the DREADFRAME Studio.

# 3. DESIGN LANGUAGE

The UI should feel cinematic, elegant, atmospheric, and premium.

Avoid cheesy Halloween design.

Do NOT overuse:

* blood splatters
* skulls
* horror fonts
* glowing red buttons
* stereotypical Halloween imagery

Instead use:

* near-black backgrounds
* subtle warm grey surfaces
* restrained crimson accents
* film grain
* cinematic photography
* high-contrast typography
* large editorial headlines
* soft borders
* atmospheric gradients
* subtle texture
* slow transitions

Visual inspiration:

* independent A24-style horror
* film editing suites
* screenplay applications
* premium creative AI tools
* cinematic title sequences

The interface should remain highly usable.

Use generous spacing.

Cards should have subtle borders and layered dark surfaces.

Animations should be understated and cinematic.

# 11. STORY STRUCTURE

Generate the story as five major sequences.

Use a model approximately resembling:

### Sequence 1 — Normality Disturbed

Establish the character and introduce an anomaly.

### Sequence 2 — Pattern

The protagonist investigates and discovers the anomaly follows rules.

### Sequence 3 — Escalation

The horror becomes personal and increasingly unavoidable.

### Sequence 4 — Revelation

The protagonist discovers the true nature of the horror.

### Sequence 5 — Confrontation

The protagonist makes an irreversible choice.

Do not force every story into identical beats.

Allow the AI to alter structure based on horror genre.

---

# 25. IMPORTANT UX PRINCIPLES

Do not make the application feel like a long form.

Reveal complexity progressively.

Prioritize visual storytelling.

Whenever possible, let users click on generated content and manipulate it.

Make regeneration contextual.

Users should be able to regenerate:

* one theme
* one character trait
* one story beat
* one sequence
* one scene
* one shot
* one image

without destroying the remainder of the story.

Always preserve user-edited content unless they explicitly regenerate it.

---

# 26. PRIVACY

Camera and photo features involve sensitive user media.

Design accordingly.

* Camera access must require explicit permission.
* Clearly indicate when the camera is active.
* Never imply images are stored permanently unless storage is actually implemented.
* Give users a clear way to remove uploaded/captured images.
* Do not silently reuse personal photographs outside the active project.

---

# 27. RESPONSIVE BEHAVIOR

Desktop should provide the full creative studio.

Tablet should retain storyboard functionality.

Mobile should provide simplified navigation and remain usable for:

* generating a project
* capturing a photo
* reviewing sequences
* viewing storyboard frames

Do not simply shrink the desktop interface.

# 29. MVP SUCCESS CRITERIA

A new visitor should be able to:

1. Arrive on an impressive horror-themed landing page.
2. Click "Create a Nightmare."
3. Enter the Studio.
4. Upload an image, capture a photo, enter an idea, or choose Surprise Me.
5. Receive several horror concepts.
6. Select one concept.
7. Receive a Story Bible.
8. See a protagonist and character arc.
9. See the story divided into sequences.
10. Expand sequences into storyboard shots.
11. View storyboard cards.
12. Generate or mock visual storyboard frames.
13. Edit or regenerate individual creative elements.
14. Explore alternate horror directions and endings.

The final result should feel like the beginning of a real commercial creative product, not a hackathon prototype.

---

# 30. FINAL DESIGN TEST

Before considering the MVP complete, inspect every major screen.

Ask:

* Does this feel cinematic?
* Does it feel premium?
* Is the information hierarchy obvious?
* Is the screen visually interesting without becoming cluttered?
* Can the user understand what to do next?
* Does the experience feel like directing a horror story rather than filling out an AI form?

Fix anything that fails these tests.

Then ensure the entire primary flow works end-to-end.
